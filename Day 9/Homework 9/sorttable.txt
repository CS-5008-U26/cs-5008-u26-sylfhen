note:
1. All numbers n are approximations, trying to get as close as possible to 10s
2. Bubble Sort and Selection Sort are exeptionally faster on sorted data
than on random or reverse. This is due largely to their early-exit/early break
optimizations. The N is so large it becomes impractical and takes up 
significant memory.

Quick Analysis:
Selection sort is fairly consistent across due largely to the same n(n-1)/2
comparisons regardless of input order.

Merge sort O(n(logn))is faster and needed a higher N to reach 10s on sorted and reverse
data. most likely from CPU branch prediction handling 
the very regular "always take from 
the left run" / "always take from the right run" comparison pattern 
better than random data's unpredictable branching.

Quicksort spread is widest due to the implementation's fixed last element pivot
degrading to O(n^2) on already ordered input.

Free vs No Free BST differences was negligible because the O(n^2) 
on a degenerate tree dominates the runtime that the O(n) freeing cost 
becomes negligible by comparison.
